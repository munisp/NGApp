### 2.3 Independence

The DPO operates with full independence in accordance with GDPR Article 38:

- Reports directly to the highest management level (CEO or Board of Directors)
- Does not receive instructions regarding the performance of DPO tasks
- Cannot be dismissed or penalized for performing DPO duties
- No conflict of interest with other roles or responsibilities
- Adequate resources provided to perform DPO functions

## 3. Responsibilities

### 3.1 Core Responsibilities (GDPR Article 39)

**Monitor Compliance:**
- Monitor compliance with GDPR and local data protection laws
- Review and update data protection policies and procedures
- Conduct regular compliance audits
- Track and report compliance metrics

**Advise and Inform:**
- Advise the organization and employees on data protection obligations
- Provide guidance on Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs)
- Train staff on data protection requirements
- Issue data protection alerts and advisories

**Cooperate with Supervisory Authority:**
- Act as contact point for the supervisory authority
- Consult with supervisory authority on high-risk processing
- Facilitate supervisory authority inspections and audits
- Report data breaches to supervisory authority

**Act as Contact Point:**
- Serve as contact point for data subjects
- Handle data subject rights requests
- Provide information on data processing activities
- Address data protection concerns and complaints

### 3.2 Additional Responsibilities

**Data Protection Program:**
- Develop and maintain data protection program
- Establish data protection governance framework
- Define data protection roles and responsibilities
- Create data protection roadmap and strategy

**Risk Management:**
- Identify and assess data protection risks
- Recommend risk mitigation measures
- Monitor implementation of risk controls
- Report residual risks to management

**Vendor Management:**
- Review Data Processing Agreements (DPAs)
- Assess vendor data protection practices
- Monitor vendor compliance
- Manage vendor data breaches

**Incident Response:**
- Lead data breach response efforts
- Coordinate breach investigations
- Determine breach notification requirements
- Prepare breach reports for authorities

**Documentation:**
- Maintain records of processing activities
- Document DPIAs and consultation outcomes
- Maintain data breach register
- Prepare compliance reports

## 4. Authority

### 4.1 Decision-Making Authority

The DPO has authority to:

- **Approve or Reject**: Data processing activities that pose high privacy risks
- **Halt Processing**: Stop processing activities that violate data protection laws
- **Require DPIAs**: Mandate Data Protection Impact Assessments for high-risk processing
- **Escalate Issues**: Escalate data protection concerns to executive management or board
- **Access Information**: Access all data, systems, and documentation necessary for DPO duties

### 4.2 Reporting Authority

The DPO reports to:

- **Primary**: Chief Executive Officer (CEO)
- **Secondary**: Board of Directors (quarterly reports)
- **Functional**: Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) for security matters

The DPO has direct access to the CEO and Board of Directors without intermediaries.

### 4.3 Budget Authority

The DPO has authority over the data protection budget, including:

- **Training**: Data protection training for all staff
- **Tools**: Privacy management software and assessment tools
- **Consulting**: External legal and technical consultants
- **Certifications**: Professional certifications and memberships
- **Audits**: Third-party privacy audits and assessments
